A 15-year-old girl was trapped inside a stranger's apartment… and instead of panicking, she started memorizing everything.
Her name was Kara Robinson. June 2002, South Carolina. She was watering flowers in a friend's front yard.
A man walked up, pretending to sell magazines. Then he pressed a gun to her neck.
He forced her into his car… and locked her inside a plastic storage tub in the back.
He drove her to his apartment. And for the next 18 hours, she was his prisoner.
But Kara made a decision. She would stay calm. Act cooperative. And study everything.
She left her fingerprints on every surface she could reach. If she didn't survive, she wanted proof she was there.
She memorized the room. The objects. Every detail about him.
Then, in the middle of the night, he fell asleep.
Kara slipped one hand out of her handcuffs… quietly freed herself… and ran.
She burst outside, flagged down a passing car, and was rushed to the police.
And she didn't just tell them what happened. She told them exactly where to find him.
Police raced to the apartment… but the man was gone.
His name was Richard Evonitz. Three days later, police chased him down in Florida. The chase ended with his death.
Then investigators searched his apartment… and made a horrifying discovery.
Evidence linked him to the murders of three girls in Virginia years earlier. Cases that had gone cold.
Kara didn't just save herself. She solved a serial killer's crimes. And it all happened because…
