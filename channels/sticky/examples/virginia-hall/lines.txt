The Nazis called her the most dangerous Allied spy in France… and she was doing it all on a wooden leg.
Her name was Virginia Hall. An American woman who dreamed of becoming a diplomat.
But in 1933, a hunting accident in Turkey changed everything. Her left leg had to be amputated below the knee.
She got a prosthetic leg. And she gave it a name… Cuthbert.
The US State Department rejected her because of it. So when World War Two broke out, she found another way in.
Britain's secret spy agency recruited her. And in 1941, she went undercover in Nazi-controlled France… posing as a newspaper reporter.
Behind the scenes, she built spy networks. Helped prisoners escape. Sent secrets back to London.
Soon, the Gestapo was hunting her. Wanted posters went up. They called her "the limping lady."
One Gestapo leader reportedly said: "She is the most dangerous of all Allied spies. We must find and destroy her."
In 1942, they almost caught her. So she escaped on foot… over the snowy Pyrenees Mountains. On her prosthetic leg.
During the climb, she radioed London: "Cuthbert is giving me trouble."
London, not knowing who Cuthbert was, replied: "If Cuthbert troublesome, eliminate him."
But she didn't quit. In 1944, she went back into France.
This time, she dyed her hair grey, and disguised herself as an old farm woman… shuffling her walk to hide the limp.
She trained hundreds of resistance fighters, blew up bridges, and helped prepare for the Allied invasion.
After the war, she became the only civilian woman in World War Two to receive America's Distinguished Service Cross.
